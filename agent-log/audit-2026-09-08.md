## 2. `advertising-revenue-sales-efficiency-2026` — Advertising Revenue & Sales Efficiency Growth Diagnostic

| # | Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Industry relevance | Yes | Digital Advertising / Sales Ops / BI; `targetIndustryRole` and 4 named stakeholders (Commercial Leadership, Sales Managers, Account Executives, Marketing Analysts) with distinct needs. |
| 2 | Depth | Yes | `businessInterpretation` explains why a revenue gap "is not a single-metric problem" and what differs between advertiser situations that look similar on revenue alone. |
| 3 | Thought process visible | **Partial** | `methodology` describes *what* was built (SQL layers, DAX measures, action rules) more than *alternatives considered*. Compare project 1's "Model choice," which names and rejects two specific alternatives — this page doesn't say why SQL Server/Power BI over another stack, or why these six lifecycle rules over a different rule set. `hypothesisVsReality` does show one real pivot (dropped a simple sales leaderboard because it confounded portfolio size with performance) — that's genuine thought process, just not repeated at the tooling level. |
| 4 | Defendability | **Partial** | Reconciliation is strong and specific (exact dollar/impression/click/conversion figures tie out across source → SQL → Power BI) — that part is fully defensible. But the *action rules themselves* (Rescue/Reactivate/Optimise/Upsell/Onboard/Maintain thresholds) aren't shown with the reasoning behind where each threshold was set, so "why does this advertiser get Rescue and not Reactivate" isn't fully answerable from the page as written. |
| 5 | Trade-offs | **Partial** | Same gap as #3 — no stated trade-off for the tool/architecture choices themselves. The `nextSteps` item "Validate rule thresholds with sales and finance stakeholders" implicitly admits the thresholds are a first pass, but that's framed as future work, not as a trade-off made now. |
| 6 | Independence | Yes | The source dataset only supplied advertising-performance records; the advertiser/rep/opportunity/activity/target layer was self-designed and generated to make sales-efficiency analysis possible at all — a real example of scope self-definition, not filling in a tutorial template. |
| 7 | Ambiguity / limitations stated | Yes — this is the page's strongest section | A dedicated "Synthetic-data disclosure" callout states plainly that advertisers, reps, opportunities, activities and targets are all fictional, and explicitly warns findings "must not be interpreted as evidence about TikTok or another real advertising platform." Plus a 4-item `limitations` list and a "Dead Ends" (`deadEnds`) section. |
| 8 | Implementation / production thought | Yes | `nextSteps` names concrete production concerns: row-level security, tracking acceptance/realised outcomes, threshold validation with stakeholders, "a governed refresh and deployment process." |
| — | Data source honesty | Yes — exemplary | States the base dataset is real but synthetic-in-content ("public synthetic digital-marketing dataset"), and is explicit that the supplementary advertiser/opportunity layer she generated herself is also synthetic. This is the clearest instance on the site of the "never overstate" hard rule in practice. |

**Result: meets 6 of 8 fully, 2 partially (#3 thought process, #5
trade-offs).** Both partial items have the same root cause: the page justifies
*what* was built and *why the business problem needed it*, but not *why this
specific tool/rule design over a plausible alternative*. Given `year: 2026`
this looks like the newest page on the site, which may explain why it hasn't
had the same pass applied as the 2019 write-up.

**Suggested fix (not actioned — a content decision for Eileen):** add one or
two sentences to the methodology or dead-ends section naming a rejected
alternative for the SQL/BI stack or the lifecycle-rule design, the same way
the ecommerce page does for its model choice. This is a small, targeted
edit, not a rewrite — flagging it rather than doing it since it touches
case-study content and the hard rules require asking before rewriting
project narrative.
